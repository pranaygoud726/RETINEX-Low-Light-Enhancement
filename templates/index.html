How Retinex works

Retinex theory (Land and McCann) models what a camera records as the product of two things: the light on the scene and how much each surface reflects.

I(x,y) = R(x,y) · L(x,y)

  1. Log transform. Taking the logarithm turns the product into a sum: log I = log R + log L.
  2. Estimate illumination. L changes slowly across an image, so a Gaussian blur of I approximates it.
  3. Subtract. log R = log I − log(Gσ ∗ I). Uneven lighting cancels out and reflectance remains.
  4. Rescale. The log output is clipped at its outer percentiles and stretched to 0–255 for display.
